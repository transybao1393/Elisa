# Date questions Elisa works out herself (`situation::date_part`): the days until or since a day, the day of the week
# of a day, the date a number of days from today. Her result goes in the turn as an exact fact and the reply's number
# is put right (a small model said "30 days" to Christmas on 11 Nov). Fix a missed wording here, not in code.
#
# One section per language; every list is "a | b | c". Keys:
#   until      asks for the days left until a day        since      asks for the days gone since a day
#   weekday    asks for the day of the week of a day      from now   follows "N" in "N days from now"
#   months     January … December, ";" between months (each "name | short name"); "{n}" stands for the month's number
#   day first  "yes" when a date in digits is day/month ("25/12"), else month/day ("12/25")
#   date       how Elisa writes a date: {d} {m} {y}, {month} (the first month name, capitalised)
#   weekday say  Elisa's own answer to a weekday question whose reply names no real day: {name} {day} {date}
#   relative   words naming a day by its distance from today, "word:days" ("tomorrow:1"); one next to a date that is
#              another day is dropped ("tomorrow, November 6" read on 11 Nov becomes "on November 6")
#   spans      words naming a time by a span, not a day ("this weekend", "next week"; "cuối tuần"): with `relative`,
#              a day phrase that, where a held file's line names a weekday, gives another time
#              (`reply_check::held_contradictions_fixed`)
#   on         the word put before a date in place of a dropped one
#   ahead      words saying an event is still to come, before its verb ("will", "is set to"; "sẽ")
#   ongoing    words before a verb's -ing form saying it is happening now ("is" launching)
#   verbs      "base, -s form, -ing form, past" per verb, ";" between verbs: next to a date before today, "will
#              launch", "is launching" or "launches" becomes "launched" ("Apple is launching … on November 6, 2026"
#              read on 11 Nov). A language without verb forms uses `done` in place of the `ahead` word instead
#   done       the word for "already happened" that takes an `ahead` word's place ("sẽ ra mắt" → "đã ra mắt"):
#              "with accents | without"
#
# [holidays]: "MM-DD = name | name": days on the same date every year, in any language.
# [moving]: days whose date changes from year to year ("Easter", "Tết"): Elisa asks the user which date they mean.
